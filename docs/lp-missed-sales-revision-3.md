# Revision 3 — `/lp/missed-sales`

Replaces **Section 6.5 ("What you get")** of the original brief. Everything in Revision 2 stands.

---

## The problem

Stallside has a lot of features. The current section lists six of them flat, at equal weight, which does two unhelpful things: it competes with the hook for attention, and it makes a deep product look like a shallow one by showing an arbitrary slice of it.

A single-hook landing page needs the hook to dominate. But feature depth still does real work here — it's what separates "a QR code generator" from "software worth signing up for." The answer isn't to list more or list less. It's to **split the section into two tiers with deliberately different visual weight.**

---

## Tier 1 — the loop

Three items. Prominent, generous spacing, the visual anchor of the section. This is the product in one breath.

- **They pay however they want** — cash, PayID, card, Apple Pay, Google Pay
- **You know instantly** — an alert on your phone the moment something sells
- **Stock looks after itself** — counts drop automatically, and you're warned before you run out

These three are the loop: pay, alert, stock. Everything else in the product hangs off them. Nothing else belongs at this weight.

---

## Tier 2 — the depth

Visually quieter — smaller type, tighter spacing, no icons, clearly subordinate. It should read as an aside, not a second pitch.

**Heading:** *And a fair bit more.*

**Body:**

> Pre-orders with a collection day, so you know how much to bake before anyone turns up. Restock emails to the regulars who asked to hear. Your own logo and colours on the stall page and the poster. A running count of everyone who'd have paid by card, if you haven't switched card payments on yet.
>
> Plus a good deal more as you grow into it.

**Closing line, set apart:**

> All of it's on the free plan.

---

## Why these four

Not arbitrary. Each was picked because it's *surprising* — something a visitor wouldn't assume a QR checkout tool does:

- **Pre-orders with collection days** signals real depth. Nobody expects order management from a QR poster.
- **Restock emails** turn a one-off sale into a repeat customer, which is a business argument, not a feature.
- **Branding** answers an unvoiced objection about looking generic.
- **The card-demand counter** quantifies the exact loss the headline names, and no competitor does it.

The vaguer trailing line only works because what precedes it is concrete. If the examples are generic, "a good deal more" reads as padding. Keep the specifics specific.

---

## What not to do

- **Don't write "plus a bunch of other features."** Vague on its own is a claim the reader can't picture and won't believe.
- **Don't build a feature grid.** Twelve cards with circle icons is the failure mode this revision exists to prevent — it flattens everything to equal importance and buries the hook.
- **Don't add an accordion or "see all features" expander.** That's a homepage device. Anyone who wants the full list can reach it from the pricing link.
- **Don't move anything from Tier 2 up.** The temptation is real because the features are genuinely good. Resist it — three is the number.
